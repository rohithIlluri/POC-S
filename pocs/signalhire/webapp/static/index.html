The five detectors

01 / FORENSICS

Toolchain fingerprints

Auto-apply pipelines export PDFs through react-pdf, Puppeteer, pdfmake, headless Chrome. Humans use Word, Google Docs, LaTeX. The producer chain, single-shot timestamps and seconds-before-submit generation all leave marks.

02 / LAYOUT

Template swarms

Wrapper tools emit structurally identical documents even when the text differs. A structure-only hash — fonts, sizes, column geometry, never content — catches fifty "different" applicants on one rig.

03 / HIDDEN

Hidden text & prompt injection

White-on-white keyword stuffing, 1pt fonts, off-page text, and instructions aimed at whatever AI reads the resume next (ignore previous instructions…). Deterministic — these are facts about the file, not judgements about the writer.

04 / JD MIRROR

Job-description mirroring

Auto-tailoring bots ingest your posting and hand it back. Rare-term overlap near 90% and verbatim five-word runs lifted from the JD are properties of the match — never of the writer's fluency.

05 / POPULATION

Recycled identities & spray

One resume looks fine; the population doesn't lie. MinHash clustering over identity-masked bodies exposes the same document under different names — the strongest fraud signal the engine produces.

06 / MEMORY

The population outlasts the batch

Submit two applications per requisition and every batch-level signal goes quiet. Your account remembers what it has scanned — one-way hashes of the body, layout, contact handles and phrasing, never text — so a rig that trickles is still a rig, and every scan you run makes the next one sharper.

    What this catches — and what it doesn't

    Cluely, Parakeet & co: the application side

    Interview-copilot workflows don't start at the interview. They start with auto-tailored resumes, one-click mass applies and recycled documents — and those leave exactly the artifacts this engine reads: generator toolchains, JD mirroring, fresh-generation timestamps, template swarms and identity recycling across your requisitions.

    What a document engine cannot honestly claim is to detect a screen-overlay assistant running on a candidate's machine during a live call. That is an interview-side surface — proctoring signals, response-latency analysis — and it's on the roadmap as a separate product stage, not smuggled into this one.

    Built to be defensible

    No signal reads writing style, fluency or "AI-ness" — the engine is blind by construction to who wrote the words. Candidate PII is hashed at the parse boundary. Weak signals can never flag an application alone, and the release gates hold the false-flag rate on verified-human resumes under 2% with a fairness slice that must stay statistically flat.

    Every signature in the database is versioned, and every score is reproducible against the signature DB that produced it — so a flag from six months ago can still be explained in an audit.
